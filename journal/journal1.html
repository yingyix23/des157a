Best Practice for Form Design

Octover 5, 2026

After reading Best Practices for Form Design,I learned several things that changed how I think about forms. One point is how to distinguish optional and required fields. I am still a little confused about deciding what information should actually be required. When I design a form, I naturally want users to complete every question. However, this reading made me think more carefully about whether every piece of information is really necessary. Asking for less information may make the form easier and faster to complete.

Another useful tip is placeholder text. Placeholder text can guide users by showing what kind of information they should enter. I have experienced forms where I started typing and then forgot what information the field was asking for. Sometimes I had to delete what I entered to check the instruction again. This made me realize that instructions and labels need to remain clear while users are completing a form.

I was also interested in the section about action buttons. The Amazon example helped me understand how a button should be noticeable and clearly describe what will happen after clicking it. Instead of a general word like "Submit," a descriptive button can help users feel more confident about their next action.

Google Forms is a good example of these practices. From my experience, its layout is simple and easy to follow. Required questions can be clearly marked, different question types are visually organized, and the form guides users when required information is missing. These details make the form easier to understand and complete.
